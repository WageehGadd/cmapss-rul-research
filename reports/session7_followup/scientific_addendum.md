# Scientific addendum to the Session 7 report

Applies to `reports/session7_main_experiment_report.md`, figures 17-21 and `artifacts/session7/` as
published in `21ec708`. None of them is edited.

## Permutation ranking: selection bias

The Session 3 permutation ranking was computed from the validation labels of the same 20 FD001
validation engines that score the Session 7 conditions. Every permutation result is therefore
selection-contaminated. **Nothing in Session 7 shows that permutation-based selection generalizes.**
Evidence would need engines that played no part in any selection, with the ranking rebuilt without
them. That was not done.

Where it applies:

| Published location | Finding | How to read it |
|---|---|---|
| Section 1, answers 2 and 5 | Beats the random mean in 24/24 pairs; within 5% at k=10 (random forest, gradient boosting, GRU) and k=7 (TCN) | Descriptive on the engines used for selection |
| Sections 7, 12 | Permutation rows and mean relative increase | Contaminated; architecture order that includes them is too |
| Sections 8, 9 | Lower degradation than random and than correlation | Descriptive; no test of permutation against correlation was planned |
| Section 10 | 24/24 below the random mean, 13 below all five draws | The pairs share engines and subsets, so they are not 24 confirmations; 0/24 significant after Holm |
| Section 11 and figure 20 (no caveat beside them) | Permutation columns of the degradation heatmap | Contaminated |
| Section 15 and figure 21 (no caveat beside them) | Permutation rows: 9/24 differ from the full set after Holm; 0/24 from the random mean | The tests do not account for selection |
| Section 14 and figure 18 | A permutation condition (GRU, k=10) is on the front | Its RMSE position is contaminated, its latency position is timing noise |
| Figures 17 and 19 | Permutation curve or markers below the random draws | The figures carry no caveat; apply this one |

Also:

- Correlation subsets (training data only) beat the random mean in 2 of 24 pairs.
- No ranked-against-random test is significant (0 of 48). A general advantage of informed selection
  is not established.
- The permutation and correlation methods differ in kind as well as in the data used, so their gap
  does not isolate the effect of the bias.
- A non-significant difference is not equivalence.

## Other limits on every finding

One training seed; 20 validation engines; heavily overlapping windows (tests use one value per
engine); the same engines also chose architectures, hyperparameters and early stopping; FD001 only.
Within the 5% tolerance the *mean* of the random draws passes at k=10 for random forest, gradient
boosting and TCN, but only 2 or 3 of the five individual draws do.

## Corrections to published wording

- Section 16 says "latency repeatability figures" were examined after the results were seen. The
  published report contains no such figures: **repeatability was not measured for the canonical
  runs.** Repeat timings in `reports/session7_incidents/execution_conditions.json` describe the
  earlier verification execution only.
- Sections 14 and 16 say power and load were not recorded; see
  [power_latency_provenance.md](power_latency_provenance.md) for what was observed outside the
  pipeline.

Reports generated by the current code place the selection caveat in sections 11 and 15 as well and
drop the repeatability phrase. Figure code is unchanged.
